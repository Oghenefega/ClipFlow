Splits & Clip Status — the full picture

Two separate things: why deleted-middle splits feel gappy in the editor, and one clean color system for every clip everywhere in the app.

Part 1 · The gap you feel at a split

The editor never actually cuts your video

The preview plays your original recording file in a single video player. When you delete a middle piece, the file on disk doesn't change — the editor just remembers "skip that part." During playback, when the playhead reaches the cut, the app tells the player: "jump ahead to where the next piece starts."

deleted middle
PIECE 2 — kept

Jumping inside a big recording isn't instant — the player has to find the spot and decode the video there before it can show a frame. Your recordings (HEVC, long gaps between decode entry-points) make that hunt slower than average.

Your exported clips do NOT have this gap. Rendering genuinely splices the pieces together. This is a preview-only artifact. (If you ever see a gap in a rendered file, that's a different bug — tell me immediately.)

The fix — two players, instant handoff

This is how real editors (Premiere, Resolve) feel seamless: never make the visible player jump. Keep a second, hidden player already parked at the next piece.

PLAYER A
visible — playing Piece 1 right now
PLAYER B
hidden — already sitting on the first frame of Piece 2, decoded and waiting
AT THE CUT
swap them instantly — B becomes visible mid-stride, A quietly moves to park at the piece after that

The gap shrinks from "noticeable pause" to about one frame.

Part 2 · One ladder for every clip

Every clip climbs the same ladder — the dash shows the highest rung reached

You spotted the key simplification yourself: "Queue" and "Rendered" are the same moment. Every kept clip automatically appears on the Queue tab — there's no separate "send it to the queue" step. What actually moves a clip forward is: was it rendered? does it have a date? did it publish? So the ladder is:

Untouched
Not reviewed yet. Faint ghost — visible enough to count, quiet enough to ignore.
→
Approved
Kept. On the Queue tab already, but not rendered — can't publish yet.
→
Rendered
MP4 exists, waiting in the queue for a time slot. This is your "Queue" state.
→
Scheduled
Has a date & time. Matches the yellow "scheduled" dots on the Tracker.
→
Published
Posted. Same cyan as the "posted via ClipFlow" dot on the Tracker.
Rejected
Reviewed and cut. Steps off the ladder — no further work ever needed.
Yellow-for-scheduled and cyan-for-published aren't new inventions — the Tracker tab already speaks exactly this color language. The Projects tab just joins in. Orange is the only brand-new color (deep orange, picked so it can't be confused with yellow at dash size).

Same colors on the tags. A clip shows the tags it has earned:

Approved Rendered Published
Rejected

Why you never saw a "Scheduled" tag before

One exists in the code — but its on-switch was wired to the Tracker's history, and the Tracker only records a clip after it publishes. So while a clip sat scheduled and waiting, the tag's condition could never be true. It only worked for the odd re-post of something already published.

The fix: the tag reads the clip's own scheduled date directly. Scheduled = the clip has a date. Published = it's in the Tracker's history. Simple, and it can't drift out of sync again.

Part 4 · "Dequeued" — the invisible state you asked about

It's what the "Remove" button on a Queue card does

On the Queue tab, every queued clip has a Remove button (and the trash icon offers "Remove from queue"). Clicking it doesn't delete anything — the clip and its rendered file stay in the project — but internally the clip gets marked "dequeued": it leaves the queue and stops counting as approved.

The problem: today a dequeued clip becomes invisible. On the Projects tab it looks identical to a clip you never reviewed — same ghost dash, no tag. You once watched it, kept it, maybe rendered it… and the app shows no trace of any of that.

My suggestion: give it a small neutral tag on its clip row — Removed from queue — keep the ghost dash on the project card, and have it block Done (it needs a decision: re-approve it or reject it properly).

Your call: does that treatment work, or would you rather a dequeued clip simply drop back to plain "Untouched" (no trace, doesn't block anything)?
